## English Version (~510 words)

**Title:** Data Monetization and Ethical Responsibility: Lessons from the Octopus Card Scandal in Hong Kong's Digital Economy

The study of academic integrity and business ethics has made me realise that in the digital economy, technical knowledge alone is insufficient without a strong ethical foundation. One of the most striking examples in Hong Kong is the 2010 Octopus Card data scandal, which remains highly relevant to contemporary debates on data monetization and platform responsibility.

In 2010, Octopus Rewards sold the personal data of nearly two million customers to six business partners for direct marketing purposes without obtaining proper consent, generating approximately HK$44 million in revenue. This case clearly violated the principles of informed consent and purpose limitation under the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (PDPO). From an ethical perspective, it breached both deontological obligations — the duty to respect individuals' autonomy and privacy — and stakeholder theory, as the company prioritised short-term profits over the interests of its customers and public trust.

This incident highlights a fundamental tension in digital economics: the conflict between data as a valuable economic asset and data as an extension of personal rights. Large platforms and data holders often justify extensive data collection through utilitarian arguments — claiming that aggregated data improves services and efficiency for the majority. However, as the Octopus case demonstrated, such practices can erode public trust and lead to long-term reputational and regulatory damage.

In the context of Hong Kong's growing digital economy and Web3 ambitions, this lesson is particularly important. As future professionals entering fintech or platform businesses, we must advocate for ethical data governance that goes beyond mere legal compliance. This includes implementing genuine consent mechanisms, ensuring transparency in data usage, and considering the broader societal impact of data-driven business models.

In conclusion, academic integrity in the digital age extends beyond avoiding plagiarism in academic work. It requires us to apply the same standards of honesty, accountability, and respect for others when designing or managing digital systems. The Octopus scandal serves as a reminder that ethical lapses in data handling can have lasting consequences for both businesses and society.

## 中文版（約 510 字）

**題目：** 數據變現與倫理責任 —— 從香港八達通數據醜聞看數字經濟的倫理挑戰

修讀學術誠信與商業倫理課程，讓我深刻體會到在數字經濟時代，單靠技術知識並不足夠，必須建立堅實的倫理基礎。香港 2010 年的八達通數據醜聞，至今仍是數據變現與平台責任討論中最具代表性的案例。

2010 年，八達通獎賞計劃在未經客戶明確同意的情況下，將近 200 萬名客戶的個人資料出售予 6 間商業夥伴作直銷用途，賺取約 4,400 萬港元收益。此舉嚴重違反《個人資料（私隱）條例》中「知情同意」及「目的限制」原則。從倫理角度看，這不僅違反了義務論所強調的尊重個人自主權與私隱的道德義務，也違反了利益相關者理論，因為公司將短期利潤置於客戶權益及公眾信任之上。

這個案例凸顯了數字經濟中一個核心矛盾：數據作為經濟資產，與數據作為個人權利延伸之間的衝突。許多平台及數據持有者常以功利主義為由，主張大規模收集數據能提升整體服務效率。然而，八達通事件證明，此類做法最終會損害公眾信任，並引發嚴重的監管與聲譽後果。

在香港積極發展數字經濟及 Web3 的背景下，這一教訓尤為重要。作為未來可能投身金融科技或平台經濟的從業人員，我們必須推動超越法律合規的數據治理倫理，包括建立真正的同意機制、確保數據使用透明度，以及評估數據驅動商業模式對社會的廣泛影響。

總結而言，數字時代的學術誠信不僅止於學術寫作中避免抄襲，更要求我們在設計或管理數字系統時，秉持誠實、問責及尊重他人的原則。八達通醜聞提醒我們，數據處理上的倫理失誤，無論對企業或社會，都可能造成長遠損害。
