# Academic Integrity and Ethical Challenges in Digital Economics: A Reflection on Hong Kong's Context

**PolyU AF5T21 + AF5644 | Week 8 Reflection | 2026年6月**

**Word Count: ~500 words**

---

## Reflection

Throughout this course, I have come to understand that academic integrity is not merely about avoiding plagiarism, but about maintaining honesty, transparency, and accountability in knowledge creation. In the field of Digital Economics, this principle becomes even more critical.

One of the most significant ethical challenges in digital economics is the tension between **data value extraction and individual privacy**. Platforms and firms increasingly treat personal data as a key production factor. While data monetization can drive innovation and economic growth, it also raises serious concerns about consent, surveillance, and power imbalance. In Hong Kong, the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (PDPO) provides a regulatory framework, yet enforcement remains challenging in the fast-evolving digital space.

Another important issue is **platform power and fairness**. Large digital platforms often act as gatekeepers. Practices such as self-preferencing, algorithmic opacity, and unfair terms for smaller merchants can distort market competition. The ethical responsibility of platforms extends beyond legal compliance — they should also consider the broader social impact of their algorithms and business models.

Furthermore, the rise of **Web3 and token economies** introduces new ethical questions around investor protection, market manipulation, and the responsible use of decentralized technologies. Hong Kong's progressive regulatory approach (including the VASP licensing regime) reflects an attempt to balance innovation with investor safeguards.

The Capstone Project on applying ICT Smart Money Concepts with OpenClaw AI Trading Agent to Hong Kong's digital economy has reinforced my awareness of these ethical issues. As a future finance professional, I recognize that algorithmic trading systems must be designed with fairness, transparency, and accountability in mind.

In conclusion, studying Digital Economics has reinforced my belief that technical knowledge must be accompanied by ethical reasoning. As future professionals in this field, we have a responsibility to advocate for transparent, fair, and privacy-respecting digital systems. Academic integrity, in this sense, is not just an academic requirement but a professional obligation that shapes the future of Hong Kong's digital economy.

# 學術誠信與數字經濟的倫理挑戰 —— 香港視角的反思

**PolyU AF5T21 + AF5644 | Week 8 Reflection | 2026年6月**

**字數：~500 字**

---

## 反思

在修讀本課程期間，我深刻體會到學術誠信不僅是避免抄襲，更是知識創造過程中的誠實、透明與問責。在數字經濟領域，這一原則顯得尤為重要。

數字經濟中最重大的倫理挑戰之一，是數據價值提取與個人私隱之間的張力。平台與企業日益將個人數據視為核心生產要素。雖然數據變現能推動創新與經濟增長，但同時也引發同意、監控與權力不對等問題。在香港，《個人資料（私隱）條例》提供了監管框架，但在快速演變的數字環境中，執法仍面臨挑戰。

另一重要議題是平台權力與公平性。大型數字平台往往擔任「守門人」角色。自我優待、算法不透明、以及對中小商家的不公平條款等行為，可能扭曲市場競爭。平台的倫理責任不僅止於法律合規，更應考慮其算法和商業模式對社會的廣泛影響。

此外，Web3 與代幣經濟的興起，帶來投資者保護、市場操縱，以及去中心化技術負責任使用等新倫理問題。香港採取相對進取的監管態度（包括虛擬資產交易平台牌照制度），反映出在創新與投資者保障之間尋求平衡的努力。

總結而言，學習數字經濟學讓我更堅信：技術知識必須與倫理思辨同行。作為未來從業人員，我們有責任推動透明、公平且尊重私隱的數字系統。學術誠信在此不僅是學術要求，更是塑造香港數字經濟未來的專業義務。
