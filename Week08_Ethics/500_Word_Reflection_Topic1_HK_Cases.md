## English Version (~500 words)

**Title:** Learning from Hong Kong's Data Ethics Cases: Octopus (2010) and Foodpanda (2022)

Studying AF5T21 has convinced me that ethical reasoning is inseparable from professional practice in the digital economy. The Octopus Card data scandal of 2010 and the Foodpanda/Deliveroo exclusivity case of 2022–2023 offer two illuminating Hong Kong cases that demonstrate why ethical frameworks matter.

In 2010, Octopus Rewards sold nearly 2 million customers' personal data (including ID card numbers) to six commercial partners for direct marketing, earning HK$44 million. From a **deontological** perspective, this violated the fundamental right to informed consent, regardless of any legal grey area. From a **stakeholder theory** lens, Octopus prioritized shareholder returns while completely disregarding customer privacy interests. Although the company argued the practice was legal under outdated PDPO provisions, the ethical violation was clear. The scandal ultimately led to a PCPD investigation, the CEO's resignation, and a public awakening about data monetization ethics.

In 2022–2023, Hong Kong's Competition Commission investigated Foodpanda and Deliveroo for requiring partner restaurants to sign exclusivity agreements. The Commission applied a **justice-based** analysis, recognizing that dominant platforms using market power to lock out smaller merchants violates fair competition. This aligns with **stakeholder theory**: platforms must consider not just their own profits but the welfare of their entire ecosystem, including small restaurants unable to negotiate favorable terms.

For my Capstone Project on applying ICT Smart Money Concepts with OpenClaw AI Trading Agent to Hong Kong's digital economy, these cases offer critical lessons. Algorithmic trading systems must be designed with transparency (so counterparties understand execution logic), fairness (so no party is systematically disadvantaged), and accountability (so responsibility for errors is clear). As a finance professional, I commit to applying deontological principles — respecting user consent and data rights — even when utilitarian arguments suggest greater efficiency.

In conclusion, Hong Kong's digital economy will thrive only if we combine technological innovation with robust ethical foundations. The Octopus and Foodpanda cases remind us that "what is legal" and "what is ethical" are not always the same.

## 中文版（約 500 字）

**題目：** 從香港數據倫理案例學習：八達通（2010）與 Foodpanda（2022）

修讀 AF5T21 令我確信，倫理思辨與數字經濟嘅專業實踐密不可分。八達通 2010 年嘅數據醜聞以及 Foodpanda/Deliveroo 2022–2023 年嘅獨家協議案，係兩個極具啟發性嘅香港案例，清楚展示倫理框架嘅重要性。

2010 年，八達通 Rewards 將近 200 萬客戶嘅個人資料（包括身份證號碼）出售畀 6 間商業夥伴作直銷用途，賺取 HK$4,400 萬。從**義務論**角度，呢個行為違反咗知情同意嘅基本權利，無論當時法律灰色地帶如何。從**利益相關者理論**角度，八達通只顧股東回報，完全忽視客戶私隱權益。雖然公司辯稱喺當時 PDPO 下屬合法，但倫理上嘅違規係顯而易見嘅。事件最終導致私隱專員公署調查、CEO 辭職，並喚醒公眾對數據變現倫理嘅關注。

2022–2023 年，香港競爭事務委員會調查 Foodpanda 及 Deliveroo 要求合作餐廳簽署獨家協議嘅行為。委員會以**正義論**分析，認同主導平台利用市場權勢鎖定中小商家，違反公平競爭。呢個與**利益相關者理論**一致：平台必須考慮整個生態系統嘅福祉，包括議價能力較低嘅小餐廳。

就我嘅 Capstone Project 將 ICT Smart Money Concepts 與 OpenClaw AI Trading Agent 應用於香港數字經濟，呢啲案例提供咗重要教訓。算法交易系統必須以透明度（讓對手方理解執行邏輯）、公平性（不系統性損害任何一方）、問責性（明確錯誤責任）為設計原則。作為金融專業人員，我承諾實踐義務論原則——尊重用戶同意同數據權利——即使功利主義論點聲稱可以帶來更大效率。

總結而言，香港數字經濟要蓬勃發展，必須將科技創新同穩健嘅倫理基礎結合。八達通同 Foodpanda 嘅案例提醒我哋：「合法」同「合乎道德」並非永遠相同。
